# The world: isometric engine and direction

## Direction (decided September 2, 2026)

The world stays isometric. Luke likes the art and is fine buying more from PixelHoo (the Evergrow artist) or another pack when a biome or interior needs it. The world is no longer "navigation only": it hosts content too (per-member islands, and later user-placed sprites).

Build order, each step visible to members:

1. Viewport culling. Done September 2, 2026.
2. More space to explore: outskirts around the Capital using owned art. Done September 3, 2026.
3. Floating My Place islands. Done September 3, 2026: `worlds/island.ts` generates a member's island from their user id (seeded PRNG, nothing stored but `users.biome` and `users.island_visibility`). From the Capital the only way home is the mycelium network's Home link (later also PCs); never a town building. The Portal on the profile lands at the island doorstep; arriving by the network lands at the island shrine. The island is a pocket you leave from; the Capital stays the daily loop. Since September 25, 2026 islands and their houses start bare: the generator places only the cottage, the shrine, and the mailbox (and the house only its computer), so members fill their own places. Only the coastline still varies by owner.
4. Wilderness with tinted biomes and user-placed content sprites (geocaching for posts: the anti-feed).
5. Ports v2 interiors with PCs. Done September 4, 2026: nine building rooms plus a generated house on every island, each with a PC. The interior art was drawn for Our Place; the Evergrow pack has none.
6. Multiplayer presence, last. The seams are already in; name tags above avatars are the first visible piece.

Step 4 began September 28, 2026, when the Capital grew into Luke's hand-drawn map (below); user-placed content sprites are still to come.

**The grown Capital** (`worlds/world-map.ts`). The world members walk is 312x264: the old 104x88 Capital (`CAPITAL_CORE` in `capital.ts`, still the authoring home for the town) pasted at `CORE_OFFSET` (104, 80), with regions from Luke's map around it, each in its own module under `worlds/wilds/`: the Frostline (snow, north, Frost Shrine in the north-east), Mirewood (swamp, west, walled with openings north, west, and south-east, Mire Shrine inside), Stillwater Lake (east, water inside a walkable shore, walled, two openings on its west side), Emberwood (autumn, south-west), and the uncharted sea along the south (sand beach, Tide Shrine south-east of the lake, and an unreachable island straight south of the Capital with a decorative mushroom that is not a warp). `wilds/layout.ts` holds every coordinate; `wilds/map-builder.ts` the shared stamps (seeded noise, walls, trails that clear trees in a band wider than their dirt). The world id stays `capital`, so every link, deep link, and presence stream is unchanged. Walls are trees on every tile of a 3-tile band, so they are truly solid: `world-map.test.ts` flood-fills from spawn, proves every door, shrine, and opening is reachable, re-seals each wall's openings to prove the swamp shrine and lake shore are reachable only through them, and proves the island is out of reach. Saves from before the growth (version 3) shift by `CORE_OFFSET` for the Capital only (`iso-save.ts`).

Outside the swamp and lake walls the land is open woodland (Luke: "much more walking room"): trees gather in noise-driven groves with glades between, small trees everywhere and tall ones only at a grove's heart, because a tall canopy covers several tiles on screen. About 91% of land is walkable and nearly all of it reachable from spawn; `world-map.test.ts` fails if more than 0.5% of walkable ground is cut off (the island aside). The island sits nine rows offshore, placed six columns east of where the Old Road meets the beach so it shows down-left on screen from there, on a laptop and on a phone held upright.

**Water and sand.** Open water (water on all four sides) draws the pure-water cell. The sheet's other open-water variant has grass tufts at its tips and is kept only in ponds at most 7 tiles across, where it reads as lily pads, so the Capital's two ponds look exactly as before (`water-autotile.test.ts` checks every tile). The pack has no sand, so `sandSheet` (`terrain-tint.ts`) bakes a pale, warm copy of each beach biome's ground sheet with only the non-grass pixels changed, and `sand.ts` draws sand tiles and the grass beside them from it.

**Terrain tint** (`terrain-tint.ts`): one HSL pass per image at load turns the forest sheets into autumn, snow, dusk, swamp, and scorched. Pixel color cannot tell a tree from a wall, because Evergrow paints foliage and building shadows in the same cool teal-greens, so every `OBJECT_CATALOG` entry names its `tint` target (`nature`, `evergreen`, `building`, or `ground`) and a world carries an optional `tint` preset. `world-assets.ts` bakes both at load for the world page and the lab (`/iso-lab?world=island&tint=snow`). A world that mixes biomes carries `biomes` (`biomes.ts`): a preset list and a per-tile index grid. Ground draws each tile from its biome's sheets and each object takes its anchor tile's biome, so a tree on the snow line is snowy all over. The loader bakes only the (sheet or kind) x preset pairs the world uses; the grown Capital's three extra presets cost about 8 MB decoded (7.4 MB of ground sheets, 0.7 MB for 30 sprites). Islands keep one `tint`.

**Object culling in a big world.** `render` no longer tests every object against the view: `buildObjectIndex` (`iso-cull.ts`) buckets objects in 8x8 chunks along the two screen diagonals, cached per object list, and `objectsNearView` returns the candidates near the view in world order, bounded by the largest loaded sprite. The exact rectangle test still decides what draws, and `iso-cull.test.ts` checks the result matches a full scan in the same order. Without it the grown Capital, with about 12.7k objects before its woods were thinned, doubled frame time on a throttled phone.

## Architecture

`/world` runs `WorldCanvas` over the engine in `src/lib/game/`:

- `world-model.ts`: the `IsoWorld` document (id, optional tint and ground sheet, terrain grid, placed objects, doors, PCs, mushrooms, links, regions), `OBJECT_CATALOG` (sprite, footprint, scale, solid, tint target), Zod validation, and a source-agnostic loader. Terrain `void` is never drawn and never walkable: an island's edge is the autotiler's dirt skirt against the dark.
- `iso.ts` 2:1 projection (TILE_W 32, TILE_H 16); `forest-autotile.ts` and `water-autotile.ts` 4-edge blob autotilers; `world-object.ts` bottom-centre-anchored sprites; `character-sheet.ts` 8-direction animator (long or short hair by the avatar's `hairStyle`); `avatar-recolor.ts` per-part palette swap to the member's avatar colors; `world-assets.ts` loads and tints everything a world needs.
- `iso-collision.ts` pure collision; `iso-actor.ts` `computeIntent` then `applyMovement`; `iso-engine.ts` `createIsoState`, `update`, `render`, and the camera; `hud.ts` prompt, toast, name tag, and warp-menu chrome (`WorldMenu.tsx` draws the menus on touch screens); `iso-save.ts` per-device localStorage save, one slot per world id; `prng.ts` seeded randomness for generated places.
- `worlds/capital.ts` is the authored town (`CAPITAL_CORE`): one building and one Ports door per community slug; `worlds/world-map.ts` sets it inside the grown map as `CAPITAL`. `worlds/island.ts` generates a member's island. `worlds/interior.ts` generates a room from a short spec, `worlds/interiors.ts` holds the nine community rooms and the PC network, `worlds/island-house.ts` the generated per-member house. `worlds/lab-town.ts` is the sandbox for `/iso-lab`.
- `viewport.ts` sizes the canvas: `fitCanvas` fills the screen on touch devices and letterboxes 3:2 up to 960x640 on desktop, and `computeViewport` picks an integer device-px-per-world-px `worldScale` so pixel art stays crisp at any DPR. `render` draws the world layer at that scale and the HUD in CSS px (`ctx.setTransform(dpr, …)`), so HUD font sizes are real CSS sizes. The canvas is `box-content`: its CSS size is the drawing surface, and the border comes out of the container's space.
- A world may name its own `groundSheet`; absent means the forest sheet. The interior sheet is painted in the same cell layout, so the autotiler needs no change and `grass`/`dirt` read as floorboards/flags. Walls are objects on the room's own edge tiles: `world-object.ts` pins a sprite's lowest base pixel to its tile centre, so a wall one tile out floats half a tile clear of the floor.
- Links (`IsoWorld.links`) are warp-menu rows that leave the world; they never need discovering. The engine fires `onWorldLink` at the peak of the fade and holds black until the page navigates.

Rules that keep the multiplayer and builder seams intact:

- Positions are world-space tile coordinates. Projection happens only at render.
- `update` never touches the canvas. `render` never mutates state.
- Collision stays a pure function over the world document, so a server could run it.
- The world is data. Adding art is a catalog entry, not an engine change. `capital.test.ts` (the town) and `world-map.test.ts` (the grown world) validate the schema and flood-fill to assert every door and shrine is reachable from spawn. Keep both passing when you edit the town or the wilds.

## Ports contract

Door ids are community slugs. `/world?at=<slug>` spawns at that building's door; `at` also accepts a shrine id or a PC id and lands one tile south of it. `/world?place=me` is the member's island and `/world?place=<username>` a visit (gated by `GET /api/users/[username]/island`). Portal buttons exist only on My Place and community pages.

Since Ports v2 a door with `warpTo` opens a world instead of porting to a page, and every real door has one: a building door warps to `<slug>-inside`, an island's cottage door to `me-inside` or `<username>-inside`.

**A door opens two ways: walk up into it, or press Enter.** The auto-warp test is on the _screen_ direction of the movement intent (`intent.sy < 0`), not the tile row delta. Doors sit on the north face of whatever they open and the camera looks at that face, but the tile axes run diagonally on screen, so "east along the street" is screen down-right and would read as northward on the row axis alone — which would make both town streets impassable, since every building door sits directly on one. Enter (the A button on touch) still works and is not gated on arming, because pressing it is already deliberate.

`state.doorArmed` is what stops a door bouncing you straight back: you always arrive inside the reach of the door you came through, so a state starts disarmed and only arms once no door is in reach. A player deep-linked to a doorstep by `?at=<slug>` therefore has to step off the threshold before walking in, or press Enter. That friction is the price of the loop being impossible; do not "fix" it by arming on key release, because a held key's auto-repeat can leave a tick with no input registered, which arms and then immediately fires. The round trip closes itself with no new spawn machinery — the exterior door arrives at the room's door named `exit`, and that door warps back arriving at the exterior door's id, because `spawnAt` resolves against the destination world's doors by id. A door without `warpTo` still ports to `/communities/<id>`; the field is optional so the fallback is live.

**PCs are the third interaction kind, beside doors and shrines.** A room's PC opens a menu whose first row logs on (a community page from its building, a profile from a house) and whose remaining rows are `world.links` — links are what any terminal here can reach, which is why an interior keeps its PC network in `links` and a shrine draws on the same list. The whole world needs an account (since v0.15.2, `world/page.tsx` sends a logged-out visitor to login before anything mounts); inside it, a community room is open to every member, and a house follows its island's visit gate, with no second permission of its own. All three kinds confirm the same way: the prompt pill flashes green for 200ms (`CONFIRM_TICKS` in `iso-engine.ts`) before the door opens, the menu appears, or the walk-into fires. When more than one is in reach, `nearestTarget` keeps only the nearest, so the prompt, Enter, and walking into a door always agree; a tie goes to the door, so a door can always be walked into from its arrival tile. Enter and Space ignore keyboard auto-repeat (`input.ts`): a held Enter used to reach a menu the instant it opened after the confirm flash and pick its first row.

**Keyboard focus belongs to the world on arrival.** The game listens on `window`, so a key pressed while a link or button has focus goes to both. Clicking the navbar's 🍄 leaves focus on that link, and in-world navigations (`router.push`) never move it, so Enter at a PC flashed the prompt green and then followed the link to `/world`, dropping the player at their saved Capital spot. `WorldCanvas` now blurs whatever holds focus on every mount, and `input.ts` leaves Enter and Space alone when their target is a link, button, or field. Movement keys always reach the world. The canvas is `tabIndex={0}` so a keyboard user can Tab back into the world. It blurs rather than focusing the canvas, because a programmatic focus after a keyboard-driven door draws the browser's focus ring around the world on some arrivals and not others.

**On touch screens the menus are tiles, not canvas rows.** `WorldMenu.tsx` is a DOM overlay of tappable tiles for the PC and Mycelium Network menus; the joystick and A button hide while it is open. The engine still owns the menu: `menuView(state, world)` gives the open menu's title and rows to both the canvas menu and the overlay, and a tap arrives as `input.pick(row)`, latched until the next tick like a press and read first by `stepMenu`. Close presses Escape. Desktop keeps the canvas-drawn keyboard menu (`render`'s `drawMenus` is false only on touch). Keep the largest menu (Log on plus nine destinations) fitting a 320px-tall phone held sideways with no scrolling; the panel scrolls past that, which founding new buildings will eventually need.

**NPCs, dialogue, and Pockets.** A world may place `npcs` (`WorldNpc`: id, tile, default facing), validated like doors and solid for collision. Talking is the fourth interaction kind: the prompt reads "Talk to <name>", the confirm flash runs, the NPC turns toward the player (`npcFacing` in `IsoState`), the engine pauses, and `onNpcTalk` fires. `WorldOverlays.tsx` then owns every DOM panel that pauses the world (dialogue, Pockets, the Notebook, the note reader) from one `OverlayScreen` state in `WorldCanvas`. `pauseForOverlay` / `resumeFromOverlay` / `endNpcTalk` put the engine in and out of the paused `"dialogue"` mode. Closing an overlay consumes the pending Enter/Space in the same event: the dialogue's own listener and the engine read the same keydown, and without that the world resumed with Enter still queued and reopened the NPC it had just closed. Items are server-authoritative: `items` (a `location`, `pocket`, `mailbox`, or `desk`, with unique owner + location + slot: 10 pocket slots, 20 mailbox slots, 100 desk slots; `from_id` and `placed_at` record who left something in a mailbox and when), `notebook_pages` (unique owner + page, 10 drafts), and `npc_gifts` (a gift once per member) behind `/api/pockets`, `/api/notebook/pages`, `/api/npcs/[npc]/talk`, `/api/mailbox`, and `/api/users/[username]/mailbox`. Every item query filters by `location`, never by `slot` being set, since mailbox letters have slots too. Catalogs: `src/lib/items.ts` (kinds, icons, discardable, mailable, and each location's capacity), `src/lib/npcs.ts` (which NPC gives what, server side), `src/lib/game/npc-dialogue.ts` (every line, including `visitor` lines for someone logged out, who gets no request). Gnome sheets are eight 21x46 standing frames built by `~/Desktop/pixel_art/tools/build_gnomes.py`; Gnomie's brimless slouching cap and rounder belly come from `gnome_style.py` beside it (Gnomette keeps the witch-hat overlay). Place an NPC where you've seen it in a screenshot: Gnomette's first spot, on the pond's south bank, was walkable but hidden under the forest canopy.

**Fixtures and the mailbox.** A fixture (`WorldFixture`) is furniture you use: the fifth interaction kind, after NPCs. It is world data, validated and solid like an NPC, and confirming it pauses the engine and fires `onFixture`, which opens a panel through `WorldOverlays`. The island mailbox is the first and the house desk (below) the second. The mailbox stands at (15, 17), west of the garden path, painted the owner's `users.mailbox_color` (`MAILBOX_COLORS` in `mailbox-colors.ts`: slate, green, or blue, chosen beside the biome in Account settings), and its sprite is `mailbox_<color>` or `mailbox_<color>_flag`. Leaving or taking a letter through the API moves the item itself (the owner changes), so a letter is never copied. Visitors see `LeaveLetterPanel`, the owner `MailboxPanel`; the island gate (`requireIslandAccess` in `src/lib/islands.ts`) decides who may leave mail. The flag is runtime state (`mailboxFlagUp`, `setMailboxFlag`), because the island document is regenerated on every load: `WorldCanvas` fetches `GET /api/users/[username]/mailbox` on arrival and whenever the engine state is rebuilt, and each panel action sets it again. Place a fixture where you have checked it in a screenshot: at its first spot east of the path, a tree hid the mailbox on about a fifth of islands (moot now that islands start bare, but trees will return as members plant them).

**The desk.** Every island house has a desk (`kind: "desk"`, id `desk`) against the north wall at (9, 2), covering (8, 2) too, under the window at col 8 and clear of the doorway at col 6. It is locked in place until members can rearrange their homes. `fixtureFootprint` in `world-model.ts` gives each fixture's tiles: collision, reach (`findNearbyFixture` answers from beside either end), and placement validation all read it. A two-tile sprite can't use the bottom-centre anchor scan, so `OBJECT_CATALOG.desk` names an explicit `anchor` (see `assets/world/README.md`). The desk holds 100 items (`location: "desk"`, slots 0 to 99), shown as 10 pages of 10 in `DeskPanel`, and takes anything pockets can hold. It is owner-only by construction: `/api/desk` and `/api/desk/[id]/take` only ever touch the caller's own items, and a visitor who presses Enter gets the locked line ("Oops! It's locked...") in a `DialogueBox`, with no request. Moves between a member's own locations go through `moveOwnItem` in `src/lib/pockets.ts`.

**The armoire, outfits, and Ghost Mode.** Every island house has an armoire (`kind: "armoire"`, id `armoire`), the third fixture, one tile against the north-west wall at (2, 8), below that wall's windows and across the room from the desk; `OBJECT_CATALOG.armoire` names its anchor like the desk. Owner-only the same way: a visitor gets the locked line with no request, the owner `ArmoirePanel` (with `OutfitEditor` for changing a look). An outfit is a look: hair style (short or long), hair color, and shirt, pants, and shoes colors, in the `outfits` table (unique owner + slot, slots 0 to 4, so five at most). Skin is the one thing the armoire never changes: no control shows it, the look schema has no skin field, and `dressedIn` copies `skinTone` from the avatar as it is. The "+" on Wearing now opens the editor on what you're wearing, to wear it straight away (`POST /api/outfits/wear`, no save) or save it as a new outfit. Wearing writes the look into `users.avatar` through `wearLook` in `src/lib/outfits.ts`, so everything that draws the avatar just works, and ends Ghost Mode; a new hair style changes `avatarKey`, so `WorldCanvas` reloads the short or long sheet. The editor is only ever seeded with a look (`lookOf`), never a whole outfit row: a row's own `name` spread into the saved body once overwrote the name typed in the editor. Ghost Mode is `users.ghost`: it lasts across visits until changed at the armoire. The panel refreshes the signed-in user after a wear or a ghost change, so `WorldCanvas` re-palettes the sheet (the old art stays up until the new loads) and reads `ghost` through a ref that `render` takes as `frame.ghost`, drawing the local player and name tag at `GHOST_ALPHA`. Nobody else sees a ghost at all: the presence hub's `isVisible()` is the one filter, the routes call `presenceHub().setGhost` and `setAvatar` after saving (going ghost is a `leave`, coming back an `update`, a new outfit an `update`), and a ghost's emote returns 200 but is never kept, so it can't replay when they reappear. The avatar builder's save calls `setAvatar` too.

**Canvas reads are noisy in privacy-hardened browsers.** Firefox's anti-fingerprinting protection (and Brave's and Safari's) perturbs `getImageData`: in Luke's Firefox about 12% of pixels came back one step off, and some transparent pixels read as alpha 1. An exact color lookup missed them, so avatars showed flecks of the sheet's original pink. `recolorPixels` in `avatar-recolor.ts` snaps each pixel to the nearest sheet color within `READBACK_TOLERANCE` (the closest two paints are 10 apart), snaps alpha to 0 or 255, and makes `recolorSheet` throw when the read is mostly noise (Firefox's `resistFingerprinting`), so the loader draws the sheet in its own colors instead. Headless browsers never add this noise; reproduce it by wrapping `CanvasRenderingContext2D.prototype.getImageData` in an init script. The other readers tolerate light noise: `world-object.ts`'s anchor scan needs alpha above 8, and `terrain-tint.ts` bakes in one-step shifts nobody can see. Under `resistFingerprinting` the tint pass would bake static into tinted islands; that is known and not handled.

**Live presence (client).** Signed-in members in the same world see each other; a logged-out visitor makes no presence requests. `use-presence.ts` gives `presence-session.ts` a React lifecycle per world id; the session opens the stream (`presence-client.ts`: EventSource, defensive parsing, our own capped reconnects after an HTTP error, one toast after 15 s down and one on return), posts our position through a throttle (150 ms minimum, only on change, a 10 s keepalive, never two in flight), and keeps a roster (`remote-players.ts`). Other members are drawn 120 ms in the past, interpolated between the stamped samples either side, snapped on a jump over 3 tiles, and shown standing once their data is a second stale. WorldCanvas calls the session's `frame` once per rendered frame, which syncs everyone into `state.entities` as `remote:<user_id>` (never solid, never saved, invisible to prompts and reach) and returns a `PresenceFrame` for `render`: each member's own palette-swapped sheet (cached across worlds, 32 at most) and the emotes showing. Emotes are six ASCII-map bubbles in `emote-art.ts` (ported from `~/Desktop/pixel_art/tools/emotes.py`), painted with fillRect in the HUD layer under the prompt pill, for 3 s; keys 1 to 6 or the picker under the full screen button (the three corner buttons stay in thumb reach on a phone), only while walking freely.

**Full screen.** A button in the world's top-right corner (every device; hidden under the touch menu overlay) toggles `WorldView`'s `ScreenMode` in `world/page.tsx`. Where `document.fullscreenEnabled` is true (Android, iPad, desktop) it calls `requestFullscreen()` on the page's root `<div>`; every iPhone browser runs on WebKit, which has no Fullscreen API for pages, so there, or when a request is refused, it falls back to "focus" mode. Both modes make the root `fixed inset-0 z-[60]` and set `data-world-immersive` on `<html>`, which hides the navbar (`globals.css`). Hiding it matters: in Chrome on an iPhone the sticky, blurred navbar painted over the fixed z-60 root and hid the minimize button, which no desktop engine reproduces. The full-screen element must be that root, never the canvas: `WorldCanvas` remounts at every door, and removing the full-screen element ends full screen. Leaving `/world` unmounts the root, which ends either mode. On desktop, full screen fills the screen and `computeViewport(..., scaleUp)` keeps the classic 480x320 framing, scaled up (Luke's choice over showing more world). In Firefox and real Chrome, Esc leaves full screen before the page sees it, so closing a menu with Esc also exits; the Cancel row does not. The truly full screen iPhone path is Add to Home Screen (planned in ROADMAP.md).

## Art and licensing

- Runtime art is gitignored under `public/world/{characters,tiles,objects,items}/`. Prod serves it from R2 via `NEXT_PUBLIC_WORLD_ASSET_BASE`. After adding art, run `npm run world:upload` before deploying, or prod 404s.
- **Every `<img>` of world art takes `src={worldAsset(path)}` and `crossOrigin="anonymous"`.** Without `worldAsset` production requests art from the app's own origin and 404s (the item icons did, v0.11.0 to v0.12.0). Without `crossOrigin`, R2 answers without CORS headers, the browser caches that copy, and Chrome later serves it to the engine's CORS load of the same URL, which fails and takes the whole world down (the mailbox color picker did this to islands in v0.12.0). R2 only allows the production origin, so localhost can't reproduce either bug; reason it through, or test the mechanism with a page script on the production origin.
- Purchased packs (`assets/Evergrow_Forest_v0.5/` and the BossNelNel sheets) are never committed: their licenses forbid redistribution. See `CREDITS.md` and `assets/world/README.md`.
- Buildings are the six Evergrow Town_House sprites at 0.5 scale. Keep scales to powers of one half for crisp nearest-neighbour drawing.
- The pack has no interiors, computers, or seasonal variants, so the interior ground sheet, the wall runs, and the computer were drawn for Our Place (see `CREDITS.md`). Room furniture comes from the pack's Town_Assets set, which the town outside had not used.

## Verifying world work

Unit tests for the pure modules first. Then a Playwright screenshot of `/world` (log in as admin first) or `/iso-lab` (`?world=capital|island&tint=<preset>`). Interiors deep-link as `/world?place=<slug>-inside` and `/world?place=me-inside`, with `&at=pc` to land at the terminal. For renderer changes compare before and after; the pond animates on the frame counter, so allow a small tolerance confined to the water. Log in once and reuse the context's `storageState`: the login route allows 10 attempts per 15 minutes per IP, so a script that logs in for every scenario starts getting 429s and its navigation waits time out. Presses latch until the next tick (`input.endTick`), so `keyboard.press` and an instant `touchscreen.tap` work for Enter and the A button, but walking reads held keys: hold with `keyboard.down`, wait about 120 ms, then `keyboard.up`. Deep-link with `?at=<door or shrine id>` instead of walking; keyboard walking drifts in iso. For anything Enter or Space triggers, also run one pass the way a member arrives: load `/feed`, click the 🍄, and travel by doors and PCs from there. `page.goto` resets focus, so deep links alone hid the focused-link bug above through two rounds of testing.
